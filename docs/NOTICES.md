## Differences from the original game

**READ FIRST** · 11/13

Re-Union X works **completely offline**: no other players and nothing is sold.

Your **Story** is your calendar: each Story period lasts several weeks, and Shop, Raids, Events and Special Quests show one week of it at a time.

### Overview — offline, no other players, no purchases

Re-Union X is a fan restoration that works **completely offline**: there are **no other players** and **nothing is sold**. Some rules of the original game had to change. Here they are in short; each topic has its own notice with every detail.

### 📅 Your Story is your calendar — one week of your period at a time

How far you are in the **Story** decides which part of the original game (2016-2021) you are playing in. Each group of Story Quests matches a **period** of the original calendar, and a period usually lasts **several weeks**.

The **Shop**, **Raids**, **Events** and **Special Quests** show **one week of your period at a time**: what the original game offered in that week.

Every **Monday** (08:00 UTC) they move on to the **next week of the same period**. After the last week of the period they start again from its first week, so nothing is lost.

**Example:** Story Quests **451-475** = **Oct 13 - Nov 9, 2016**, **4 weeks**. You see Oct 13-19 first; each Monday the next week (Oct 20-26, Oct 27 - Nov 2, Nov 3-9), then Oct 13-19 again.

Clear **Story Quest 476** to reach the next period (from November 10, 2016), with its new banners, raids and events.

**Seasonal banners** (New Year, Halloween, Holidays...) follow the **real calendar** instead.

### 🔄 Resets (UTC) — daily, weekly and monthly at 08:00

**Every day at 08:00**: Daily Missions, Login Bonus, the free draw of the day.

**Every Monday at 08:00**: Weekly Missions and the new week of the Shop, Raids, Events and Special Quests. The game downloads updated data: the first loading takes longer.

**On the 1st of each month at 08:00**: the monthly Lux campaign and a data download. Raid Boss levels go back to **LV 1** at the start of the 1st (00:00 Pacific time).

### 📌 Catalogs: keep one of each — keep one past item in each Catalog

The Shop has the **Banner Catalog** as its first banner. The other catalogs (Coliseum, Raid, Event, Avatar Board, Special) are in the **Game Catalog**, the first banner of **Events**: tap **Details** (not original).

From a Catalog you can **keep one** past banner, quest, raid or event: it stays available **every week**. Keeping another one replaces it.

Only things from a period your Story has already passed can be kept. **Leave and re-enter** the screen to see it.

### ⚔️ Raids — solo, free and doubled Lux on weekends

Raids are **solo** for now. A boss you do not defeat **keeps the damage** you dealt until you beat it or it escapes.

**Monday to Friday**: raids cost their original AP. **Saturday and Sunday** (Pacific time): **0 AP** and **Bonus Time all day** with **double Lux**.

Every raid is open **all day**. A boss gains **1 level** per defeat, up to LV 99. A kept raid has a level of its own, from LV 1.

### 🎉 Events — open all day, coins never expire

Events that lasted only hours are open for their **whole original day**.

**Event Coins never expire**. Event quests never bring a Raid Boss.

### 💎 Shop and Jewels — no purchases, one free draw a day

No purchases: **Jewels** come from quests, missions, the Login Bonus and Presents.

All **Free Deals** share **one free draw a day**.

Draw rates that the original game never published are (rebuilt): each banner's Details say so.

### 👑 VIP — from Weekly Missions, locked for now

VIP is no longer a paid pass: complete **all the Weekly Missions** of a week to get it for the rest of that week and the whole next one.

For now it **cannot be earned**: the Weekly Missions include Union Cross, which is not back yet.

### 🎁 Nothing is lost — campaigns, coins and points last

Campaign rewards start when your Story reaches them and **never end**.

**Event Coins** and **Draw Points** never expire. Mission rewards you forgot to collect go to your **Presents**.

### 🧩 Reconstructed parts — lost parts rebuilt and marked

Where the original data or graphics are lost, they are rebuilt and marked (rebuilt) in the notices. The full list is in **Differences in detail**.

## Differences in detail

**DETAILS** · 10/16

Every rule that differs from the original game, topic by topic.

No purchases, and presents **never expire** (not original).

### Game and progress — offline server, max LV 999

Re-Union X is a fan restoration of KINGDOM HEARTS Union χ[Cross] that works **completely offline**: the small server the game talks to runs on your own phone or computer, nothing goes online, and your progress is saved only there.

The in-game date is set between 2016 and 2021 so that the original game keeps working. Missions reset at the real times shown in their countdowns.

Completed missions that you did not collect before they end are sent to your Presents.

The maximum LV is **999**, as in the original game from January 2021. The Lux needed for each level up to LV 304 is original; from LV 305 it is (rebuilt) from players’ videos of 2018-2021, because the original values are lost.

Limited Missions rotate among the original campaigns: one monthly Lux campaign and one short campaign each week.

### Medals — rebuilt effects, full artworks

A few Spirit parts without original graphics are replaced by a similar part.

The "Medal STR +N" and "Medal DEF +N" effects of Special Attacks and Supernovas are rebuilt from their descriptions, because the original values are lost.

The **Art** button in a Medal's details shows the original **full artwork** of the 55 Medals that had one: on every star of those families, on the SN++ reissues with the same picture (**Key Art #3**, **Key Art #8**, **Axel Art**, **Roxas Art**) and on their boosted copies.

Medals with a different picture, such as **SN++ - Terra, Ven, Aqua**, have no Art button.

**Reconstructed**: these Medals are part of an artwork and show it, although the original data gave them none: Illustrated Aqua (A, SN, SN+), Illustrated Xemnas, Sora B, Terra B, Donald B, Goofy, Marluxia [EX], Xion [EX], KH II Kairi [EX+], Supernova HD Terra, HD Ventus and HD Aqua, and the SN++ Illustrated Xemnas, Illus. Marluxia, Illustrated Zexion and Master Xehanort.

### Raid Boards — rebuilt Medals and sparkles

The **Sora & Moogle B** Medal (JP 5th anniversary, a Raid Board reward) is (rebuilt) from its description: used in a Level Up it adds 100 STR and 100 DEF, like Chip and Dale.

The Raid Board cell listed as a **6★ Fantasia Mickey A** gives the **5★ Fantasia Mickey A**: no 6★ version of that Medal is known, and the board pairs it with Magic Brooms, which evolve the 5★.

The **Starlight Ears** and **Dazzling Disco** Spirit parts (Raid Board rewards) each have one (rebuilt) texture: a small twinkling sparkle and a tiny dot were lost and are drawn with the matching sparkle of the same set.

### Shop and draws — rebuilt rates, presents for bonuses

Shop draw rates are reconstructed where the original game did not publish them (Medal rarities, guarantees and Skill rates are original): Draw Odds and each banner's Details say so.

Bonus items of a draw (Magic Mirror, Gems...) arrive in your Presents.

All Free Deals share one draw a day, and VIP Free Deals need the VIP benefit (see the Free Deals and VIP Draws notices).

Free deals that could be drawn only once (or once per day) keep that limit.

Seasonal draw currencies (Spooky Cookies, Moogle Points, Holiday Draw Tickets) are kept separately; the Shop shows one of them at a time. When you earn one of them, the reward message calls it a Draw Ticket, but it goes to its own currency.

Some 2016-17 banners only drew Epic Medals; the game data no longer says which Medals were Epic, so these banners draw from their full sets.

Blue Fairy Deal banners are in the Banner Catalog but cannot be drawn yet: Blue Fairies will come with a future update.

Trait Medals are back: a deal that guaranteed a Trait Medal gives it (all 313 Trait Medals, matched to their Medals from the community wiki).

Boosted Medals: the chance of a boosted version on deals that offered one is reconstructed (5% per eligible Medal). Guaranteed max-level Skills are given only for Skills that exist in this restoration.

Boosted stats on deals that did not state them are reconstructed (+1000 STR, +1000 DEF), and bonus support Medals earned through a guarantee arrive in your Presents.

### Seasonal and kept banners — real-calendar seasons, one kept banner

Seasonal banners follow fixed dates of the real calendar, and the Shop shows the most recent unlocked edition of each season, from the first time you log in inside its window. In the original game each edition ran on its own dates.

The banner you keep in the Shop with **Keep in the Shop** did not exist in the original game.

Limited Missions that give Platinum Tickets or Draw Tickets appear only after a banner that uses them is unlocked.

Draw Points from selling Medals start only when the Story reaches the first Draw Points Deal, and they never expire (see the Draw Points notice).

### Home banners — deals and quests of your Story week

The banners on the **Home** screen show the current **Shop** deals and the events, raids and Special Quests of your **Story week**, with the time left. Tap one to open it in the Shop or in Quests. The original lists of these banners did not survive, so which banners appear and in what order is chosen by this server (not original).

### Purchases — nothing sold, presents never expire

There are no purchases: Jewels and items come from quests, missions and presents.

The Present Box holds up to **500** presents and **500** Jewel presents, where the original held 200 (not original): campaigns that start when your Story reaches them can send many presents at once. Presents are never deleted: not for being over the limit, and **they never expire**, where the original deleted them after 30 days (not original). The expiry date shown on each present is only a placeholder far in the future.

Spirit Training slots that were bought in the original game open in other ways (not original):

Slot 2 ("Purchase Weekly Jewels"): open while you have the **VIP benefit** of the Weekly Missions.

Slot 4 ("Purchase Jewel Box E"): open once you complete **all the monthly Limited Missions** (the month's Lux campaign), for the rest of that month and the whole next one, although its label says "Valid until the end of the month".

Slot 3 keeps its original rule: 5 draws from 10-Medal Deals paid with Jewels (not Free Deals or Ticket Draws) open it for 3 days.

## Coliseum

**IMPORTANT** · 11/26

The monthly **Coliseum** seasons are back from Story Quest 130: April 2016 to March 2018 for now.

The season follows your **Story week**; each season keeps its own progress and coins (not original).

Ranking brackets became **score thresholds**; keep one past season from the **Coliseum Catalog**.

### Overview — 24 seasons, from Story Quest 130

The **Coliseum** is back in **Quests** from **Story Quest 130**: the monthly seasons of the Olympus arena with their Tiers and Rounds, the original enemies, the one-turn shortcut, Coliseum Coins, Tier Rewards, the Coliseum Boards and the ranking rewards. Sixty-three seasons are here: **April 2016 to June 2021**, the whole life of the Coliseum.

### Seasons — follow your Story week, progress kept

The season follows your **Story** like the Shop: the Coliseum plays the season of the month of your **Story week** (not original) (the original changed season on the 1st of every real month). A week that spans two months counts for the month it starts in, and when your Story week goes back to an earlier month the Coliseum goes back to that season too. In a Story week whose month has no season here yet, the Coliseum is unavailable.

Each season keeps **its own progress, Coliseum Coins and Coliseum Boards** in your save (not original). In the original game every new month started from Tier 1 with 0 coins and the coins of the old season were lost; here, going back to a season resumes it where you left it. A season played for the first time starts at **Tier 1, Round 1** with 0 coins, as in the original.

From **November 2016** a season has **eleven Tiers**, one more than before, as in the original: Tier 10 has 10 rounds and Tier 11 is the endless one.

Every season has **all the Tiers of the original data**: from **January 2017** some Tiers have **999 rounds** before the endless last one (Tiers 11-14 from January to March 2017, 11-20 in April 2017). A save in the old last Tier keeps its Tier and Round.

From **May 2017** a season has **22 Tiers**, as in the original: Tiers 11, 12 and 13 have 50, 100 and 300 rounds, then nine Tiers of 999 rounds, the last one endless.

From **June 2018** a season has **23 Tiers**: Tiers 1 to 15 have 20, 15, 15, 10 (seven times), 50, 100, 300, 500 and 500 rounds, then 999-round Tiers, the last one endless. April and May 2018 have 22 Tiers.

From **May 2019** the Coliseum was **revamped**, and so are its seasons here: **eighteen Tiers**, 4,000 rounds in all. Tier 1 has 200 rounds, Tiers 2 to 7 have 400, Tier 8 has 300, Tier 9 has 200, then Tier 10 to Tier 18 have 100 rounds each. There is **no endless Tier**: when you clear **round 4,000** the season is **complete**: the Coliseum screen then says «This event is unavailable.» (the original message) while Ranking, Tier Rewards and Coliseum Boards stay open, and no round 4,001 exists (the season page of the Coliseum Catalog says «Season complete») (not original). The original had one more Tier, **Tier 19** (1,000 rounds at 99 AP): it is **not served**, as a wall made for the designers (not original).

In these seasons the enemy level is the **round number** (up to 4,000) and a month has **ten maps** until December 2019 and **nine maps from January 2020**: round 10 plays map 1 again in a nine-map month. The one-turn shortcut unlocks up to **199 rounds** at once in Tiers 1 to 3, then 149, 99 and 49 in the later Tiers. After such a clear in Tiers 1 to 4 the banner can show «99 ROUNDS UNLOCKED» (or 49 for 149) although 199 (149) rounds are opened: the banner never draws the hundreds digit (not original), to be fixed later.

Clearing **Tier 10** (round 3,200 of the season) gives the Tier title of the month, one for each season, for example **May 2019 Victor**; Tier 11 to Tier 18 pay no reward of their own.

### Rounds and AP — retry freely, original AP campaigns

As in the original game, a lost or abandoned round can be tried again as often as you like, and the AP paid to start it is not given back. Clearing every wave in one turn unlocks the next rounds at once, also across tiers; when you clear a later round, the rounds and tiers you skipped pay their coins and Tier Rewards.

Rounds cost their original **3 to 5 AP**. The Coliseum AP campaigns of 2016 are back on their original days of your Story week: **1 AP** from May 19 to 31 and from June 16 to 22, 2016, and **0 AP!** on November 1-3, 2016, for the Coliseum season being played: the November 2016 season, unless you keep another one.

From **May 2017** every round costs **0 AP**, as in the original data.

Each Tier of the revamp pays its Coliseum Coins when you complete it: 100 in Tier 1, 300 in Tiers 2 to 4, 500 in Tiers 5 to 7 and 700 in Tiers 8 and 9. Tiers 5 to 7 also pay 10 coins a round and Tiers 8 and 9 pay 30 a round.

The «1 AP» rate is (rebuilt): this version of the game rounds the original rate up to 2 AP on a round, so the rate is adjusted to keep 1 AP.

From **April 2018** each Tier also pays its Coliseum Coins **in one sum** when you complete it (9,000 in April 2018, 8,100 from May 2018); the rounds still pay 1 to 10 coins until November 2018. A one-turn clear can skip up to **99 rounds**: when you clear the later round, every Tier you completed pays its sum once. From **December 2018** a round pays **no coin** at all.

The Tier Rewards of these seasons include the **Magic Broom**, the **Fantasia Mickey B** and the **Moon Gem**.

From **December 2018** the Coliseum Boards add the **Tier 11, 12 and 13 Bonus** boards, as in the original.

### Ranking — brackets became score thresholds

There is only one player on this server, so the monthly ranking cannot be a real ranking. Each reward bracket of a season is a **score threshold** instead (not original), on the original measure: the furthest round you cleared. Reach a threshold and its rewards go to your **Present Box**, once; the Ranking screen lists the thresholds.

The thresholds are (rebuilt): the only worldwide cutoffs on record are those of **August 2016** (recordings of its ranking). August to October 2016 use values read from them; the other seasons are interpolated from them and adjusted to their number of rounds (from November 2016, ten more rounds: every threshold is 10 rounds higher).

From **May 2017** each month uses the border ranks posted by players at its end (r/KingdomHearts, 2017): its thresholds follow those values. August 2017 and January to March 2018 have none and use the curve of the whole 2017 era.

About 100,000 players a month were ranked in 2017, so almost everyone was in the top 100,000: from May 2017 the **50,001st-100,000th** bracket and the lower ones need only **1 round**.

The rewards of each bracket are the original ones: from the game data and the notices of their season up to November 2016, from the community wiki from December 2016. The December 2016 **Pegasus Knight Helm** goes to the top 10,000, as its notice says. The **December 2017** Santa Hat, Beard and Brows are **Spirit parts**, as its notice says.

From **April 2018** the border ranks are rare: April and June 2018 and April 2019 use the few ranks posted by players for that month, the other months the curve of the whole 2018-2019 era (rebuilt). Most months have **eight** reward brackets; **December 2018** has **nine** reward brackets (Gems after the 27 December 2018 increase) and **March and April 2019 twelve**; **January and February 2019** pay the **Dual Meow Wow** medals.

From **May 2019** the ranking has **twelve** reward brackets from May to September 2019; from **October 2019 to October 2020** twelve brackets with the community edges (the first eight of before, then 10,001-15,000, 15,001-20,000, 20,001-35,000 and 35,001-80,000); from **November 2020 to April 2021 sixteen** brackets (1-100 up to 30,001-80,000); **May and June 2021 two**: 1-40,000 for a **Moogle 3-star** and 40,001-80,000 for a **Moogle 1-star**. The prizes of the revamp are the featured Special Attacks of the month, medals, **Gems** (Power, Speed, Magic, Sun, Moon, Rainbow, Brilliant) and **SP Chip & Dale**; from **November 2020 to April 2021** every bracket also pays **Avatar Coins** (100 in the top brackets, down to 10), as named in the community wiki and the notices of the original game. The February 2021 top prize is the **ATK B XV Max & GA 2** Special Attack, which the game data of this server did not hold: it was added as the next step of its ladder (rebuilt).

The thresholds of these seasons are (rebuilt) and mostly **interpolated**: only a few cutoffs survive for 2019 to 2021 (the season-end points of **December 2019**, **July 2019** and **December 2020** among them) and the number of ranked players fell from about 32,000 in 2019 to under 10,000 in 2021. Where a month has no point, its thresholds follow the curve of the era, moved by the player count of that month. The lowest bracket of each season needs **1 round**; in **April 2021** the **8,001-10,000th** bracket needs 1 round too, since fewer than 10,000 players were ranked.

In May 2016 every ranked player got the same reward, as in the original: one cleared round is enough. Before your first round of May 2016 the goal reads **10000000th**: that was the original lowest bracket of that season (5,000,001st to 10,000,000th).

### Coliseum Catalog — keep one past season, filters

The **Coliseum Catalog** is (not original): it is an addition of this server, like the Event and Raid Catalogs. Open it from the **Game Catalog** banner, the first of the Events tab (its **Details**, then **Coliseum Catalog**); a season can be kept from Story Quest 130, when the Coliseum opens. It lists every season here with its tiers, AP, Coliseum Boards (with their cards) and your progress. Tap a season for its page: its Coliseum Boards with the nodes unlocked and the coins still needed, its Tiers and Tier Rewards, the score thresholds and where its maps come from.

One **past season** at a time can be **kept**: the Coliseum then plays the kept season until you **remove** it, whatever your Story week, also when your Story week goes back to an earlier month. Only past seasons can be kept: not the season of your Story week, nor a month your Story week has only just reached. Keeping or removing a season loses nothing, since each season keeps its progress and coins. A kept season shows **«Ends in»** the end of the current week (not original): it never ends on its own, but the screen needs a date.

The Catalog **year tabs** and **Filters** (not original) find seasons by game year, by month or board name, by state (season of your Story, can be kept now, kept, later in the Story), by **Coliseum Boards** all completed or not, and by **Tier Rewards** all taken or not.

The Coliseum Catalog card and the season cards are our own design (not original), made from original pieces of the game: the Olympus arena panel, the Coliseum Coin and the Tier crown.

### Maps — original, Japanese and generated maps

From **May 2019** the maps are the original ones of the Japanese version of the same month, as before. **Twenty-eight** of them lost their header file in the game data; each header was (rebuilt) from the original map data of the same map, with no enemy taken from another map.

The maps of **January 2020** are not in any surviving file: they are whole copies of the nine maps of the **May 2020** season (not original), under their own map numbers. The maps of **October 2020** are the same: each round is a whole copy of a map of another month (maps of May 2020, January 2021, July 2020 and March 2020, among them), each with its own enemies and positions (not original).

The maps of **July 2019** do not survive either: they are generated from the round lists of the community wiki (khuxwiki) with its thirteen enemies, like the 2016 and 2017 months, and their enemy buffs are dropped (not original).

The maps of **April 2018 to April 2019** use the original maps of the Japanese version. Five of them lost their header file in the game data: **October 2018** maps 1 and 5, **November 2018** map 2 and **February 2019** maps 3 and 5. Each header was (rebuilt) from the original map data of the same map, with no enemy taken from another map. **July 2018** uses the first Japanese version of its maps: the later one carried enemies of 2019.

The maps of **April to July 2016** are the original ones, from the 2016 worldwide game files. **December 2016 to April 2017**, **May 2017** and **January to March 2018** use the original maps of the Japanese version, which the worldwide game most likely reused (its round lists match them).

No map of **August to November 2016** and **June to December 2017** survives: their rounds are played on **generated maps** (not original), built from the round lists of the community wiki (khuxwiki) with the original enemies of those seasons, in the original arena.

The wiki lists enemy buffs (higher Strength or Defense) on some waves of the maps 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 10 of **August 2016**, 1 and 7 of **September 2016**, and on most maps of **June to December 2017**.

Until April 2019 the ten maps of a season repeat in every tier: rounds 1, 11, 21 … of a tier play map 1, rounds 2, 12, 22 … map 2, and so on. From May 2019 the maps repeat round after round across the whole season (nine maps from January 2020).

A map cannot carry those buffs, so these enemies fight **without them** (not original); the counter of the June 2017 Ringmaster is dropped too.

The wiki gives only the name of the boss of map 10 of **June 2017** and **September 2017**: they are the Coliseum **Ringmaster** and **Enraged Elk** of the game data.

On the eighth map of **November 2016**, three Shadows of the third wave stand on places added by the generator (not original): no original layout holds seven enemies in one wave. Eight maps of 2017 have one or two such places.

### Enemies — original rows, a few by the rule

The Coliseum enemies of 2017 and 2018 have their **original stats**: from the original Coliseum enemy table (ReLux Team donation), or a copy of the original row when a season reuses an enemy under the id of its month.

Where no original row survives (the June 2017 **Ringmaster** among them), the row is (rebuilt): derived from its original base enemy by the variant rule of the original Coliseum rows (same moves, HP raised from level 300). No value is estimated.

The rows of the Japanese version that survive only in the 2019 shape are not used before **May 2019**. **Jewel Sorceress** (June 2018, April 2019) has no surviving original row of its own: it is a copy of the Japanese Coliseum row of **September 2018**, under its own id (rebuilt).

The seasons from **May 2019** use the original enemy rows of the revamp (ReLux Team donation), among them the **6xxxx** rows made for the new Coliseum. The thirteen enemies of **July 2019** are those original rows, recovered from the same data; the round rules of the month (buffs, counters) are kept inside them.

The 14 enemies of the older game that are in the new seasons too (20007, 20011, 20012, 20015, 20016, 20017, 20035, 20059, 20071, 20110, 20131, 20136, 20137 and 20144), and the 25 that earlier seasons added, keep their **earlier row**: they fight at the level of their round, up to **LV 4,000**, with the stats they had before the revamp (not original). They appear and play normally at those levels; whether their strength keeps growing past LV 999 or stops there was not measured.

### Graphics — board cards, one button icon stands in

The card pictures of the Coliseum Boards are original art: from the 2016 worldwide files or the Japanese files where they match the board, else the board previews of the community wiki at their original size.

The cards of the **Mango Sweetie** (July 2017) and **Illusionist** (September 2017) boards are (rebuilt) from the small picture of the community wiki, the only one that survives.

## Event Quests: how they work

**IMPORTANT** · 11/16

The original **Event Quests** are back in Quests > Events, following your Story week.

Rewards are given once, drops keep their original chances, and **Event Coins** never expire.

The **Event Catalog** lists every event; keep one past event at a time.

552 events · 4,256 of 4,256 quests · 115 Event Boards

### Overview — past events following your Story week

The **Event Quests** are back: the limited-time events of the original game, with their original maps, enemies, objectives and rewards wherever their files survive. They are in **Quests > Events**, after the raids.

Events follow your Story like the Shop: each week shows the events whose original dates fall in the matching week of the original game. An event that ran for three weeks is listed in each of those weeks.

Today **552 events** are served, with **4256 of their 4256 quests** (583 events are in the data so far, from July 2016 to February 2021). The others follow in later updates: the **Event Catalog** lists every event and, for each quest not served yet, the reason (a map or an enemy whose original files are still missing). A quest whose enemy is missing is not served: no other enemy stands in for it.

### Opening hours — open for the whole original day

An event that was open for only a few minutes or hours in the original game is open here for its **whole original day** (not original): it is listed on the real weekday (Pacific time) of that day, all day. An event with one quest a day lists **all its quests for its whole period** (not original, where the original opened them one a day).

### Event Catalog — list every event, keep one past event

The **Event Catalog** is (not original): it is an addition of this server, in the style of the Special and Raid Catalogs. Open it from the **Game Catalog** banner, the first of the Events tab (its **Details**, then **Event Catalog**): it lists every event of the game with its quests.

One **past event** at a time can be **kept** in the Events tab (it shows the **KEPT EVENT** tab), beside the events of the week. Events later than your Story stay locked (**Unlocks at Story Quest N**), and events with no quest served yet are marked **Not built yet** and cannot be kept.

### Rewards — once, original drop chances

Objective rewards and first-clear items are given **once**, as in the original game. Enemy drops keep their **original chances**: an item that dropped 5% of the time still does.

For **Event Coins**, see **Event coins and boards** below. Each event's **Details** shows its original notice as it was.

Event quests **never bring a Raid Boss** and do not fill the raid gauge. This is the original rule from January 24, 2019, when Raid Bosses began to appear only in raid events; before that date some event quests could bring one, so for older events it is (not original).

The story scenes before and after some event quests are not shown yet.

### Event coins and boards — coins never expire

**Event Coins** are back, with the **Event Boards**: today **115 Event Boards** in **23 board sets**, for 51 events. The quest list of these events has the **Event Boards** button. Event Coins come from objectives (**once**) and from enemy drops (at every clear, also for a kept event).

Each **board set** (the boards of a month, shared by its events) has **one balance**, and Event Coins **never expire** (not original) (the original game lost them when the event ended, as the board screen still says). Each cell and each finished board gives its reward **once**.

One board set is listed at a time: the set of the **last event quest you started** (only a quest of an event whose Event Boards are available counts), otherwise the newest set of your Story week, otherwise the set of your **kept event**. The Event Coins shown on the boards are that set's balance; the other sets keep theirs.

In the original game every cell of an Event Board paid a reward, and here every served board pays them all, the **Booster** cells included (see **Boosters** below). A board with a cell that cannot be paid here is **not served**, nor is a board that opens after it. The sets of **November 2016 and March 2017** are not served yet (3 events): those events are served **without Event Boards**, and the Event Coins they pay are **kept** in the balance of their set, which is not shown until its boards arrive.

**Raid Coins** are unchanged: raids pay and spend them as before.

Announcement notices with no quest and no banner of their own (for example «New Event Coins Available in October!») are not events: the Event Catalog does not list them, and their boards are on the pages of the events of their set.

The **Riku & Moogle** 6★ Medal, a drop of the August and September 2019 coin events, is back: a Sell Medal with no stats, sold for **500 Jewels**, with its original picture.

### Boosters — rebuilt effects, timed from pickup

The **Boosters** are back: **428 Boosters**, paid by 49 event quests, 267 Event Board cells, 3 Raid Board cells and 79 Avatar Board cells. As in the original game, a Booster raises the damage of the Medals it names, or the slot multipliers of the Keyblade it names, until it expires: you see it in your profile (**Boosters**, with its expiry), as the light-blue **+N%** plate on the Medal Details screen, and in battle. Different Boosters add up.

No original Booster record with its effect values survives: each effect is **rebuilt from the Booster's original description** (the attribute, Upright or Reversed, the group of Medals or the Keyblade it names, and its value). The Medals of a group are the ones the original game data assigns to that Booster.

In the original every Booster expired on a fixed date. Here a Booster lasts, **from the moment you get it**, as many days as it lasted in the original from the start of its event to its expiry (**8 to 32 days**) (not original) (in the original a Booster obtained late in its event lasted less). So a Booster from a past event, a kept event or an older board set works for its full time too. For 156 Boosters whose expiry notice did not survive, the end of their event or board is used as the expiry.

Each quest objective and each board cell gives its Booster **once**, ever: a replay does not give it again, not even after it expired. The same Booster from another quest or cell extends the one you have.

A Booster reward recorded before the Boosters arrived (a quest you cleared earlier) is given at your first login after this update, with its full time.

## Raid Bosses

**IMPORTANT** · 11/06

Weekly **Raid Bosses** follow your Story; they are solo for now and keep the damage you dealt.

Monday to Friday raids cost their AP; on **weekends** they cost 0 AP with Bonus Time all day.

Keep one past raid from the **Raid Catalog**; missing graphics and values are (rebuilt).

### Overview — weekly bosses, levels reset monthly

The weekly **Raid Bosses** follow your Story like the Shop: each week shows the raids of the matching week of the original game. Their graphics, moves and strength come from the original game files wherever they survive; the parts below are (rebuilt).

Each Raid Boss has its own level: it rises by 1 for every defeat (some event raids by more, see below), up to **LV 99**, and all levels **reset to LV 1 on the 1st of every month** (00:00 Pacific time), as in the original game. A boss that comes back in a later week starts again at LV 1; a boss already on the field keeps its level until it is defeated or escapes.

### Raid AP and Bonus Time — weekdays cost AP, weekends 0 AP

On this server **one rule applies to every raid**, worldwide and Japanese alike, kept raids included, and every raid is open **all day** (not original). From **Monday to Friday** (Pacific time) each raid quest costs its **original AP** and there is **no Bonus Time**. On **Saturday and Sunday** raid quests cost **0 AP** and it is **Bonus Time all day**: the Lux of raid bosses is **doubled**, **6,000** points of the raid gauge bring a boss, and the bosses that gained more levels in Bonus Time do so on the weekend.

There are **no exceptions**: a raid quest that was free in the original game (a free week or an AP 0 campaign) costs **5 AP** from Monday to Friday (rebuilt: the usual price of a raid quest of the time), and the 24-hour Bonus Time of June 2020 is not served, since the weekends are free for every raid.

In the original game the hours were different and changed over the years: Bonus Time came four times a day (raid quests at 0 AP or less, double Lux), the Daily Raid Events and the Japanese monthly 30-minute raids opened only in their daily windows, and some weekly raids only in the Bonus Time hours. The original notices behind a raid's **Details** button show those hours and costs as they were, with a note that the rule above applies here. Each boss keeps its own level rules (a daily raid boss still starts again at LV 1 every Pacific day).

The quest top shows the weekday AP; on Saturday and Sunday the yellow «Bonus Time: Raids cost 0 AP!» balloon shows that raid quests are free.

### Playing alone — solo fights, bosses keep their damage

Raids are **solo for now**: no other player joins your fight, and nobody is simulated. Every raid can be won alone with its original HP, over several attempts.

A boss you do not defeat **keeps the damage** you dealt: it stays on the field with the HP it has left until you defeat it or its timer runs out and it escapes. The first fight costs 0 AP; each further attempt costs the AP shown.

Weak points you break stay down for **3 minutes** from the end of your latest fight, then come back with **half of their HP** (rebuilt: the sources only say weak points came back with part of their HP).

Each Raid Boss you defeat counts for the Daily, Weekly and Beginner raid missions. VIP still cannot be earned, because the Weekly Missions also include Union Cross (see the VIP Draws notice).

Defeating enemies in the Story Quests that have a raid boss (replays too) and in raid event quests fills a hidden raid gauge. At **8,600** points a Raid Boss appears; in Bonus Time (here Saturday and Sunday) **6,000** are enough. Both numbers are (rebuilt) from values in the game data and from the original rule of about 80 defeated enemies per boss. Proud, Special and other event quests do not fill the gauge, and no new boss appears while one is on the field.

Raid bosses appear again in the Story, from Quest 4 to **Quest 825** (822 quests): **Behemoth** (rare: Behemoth Ω), **Trickmaster** (rare: Trickmaster Ω), **Guard Armor** (rare: Guard Armor Ω), **Darkside** (rare: Darkside Ω), **Savage Spider** (rare: Venomous Spider), **Trident Tail** (rare: Trident Tail Ω) and **Enraged Arachnid**. After Quest 825 raid bosses appear only in raid events, as in the original game.

Their HP, moves and rewards are the original **2016** ones (for example Behemoth has 8,000 HP at LV 1), so they are weak for late quests: no later values survive. They give Medals and Mythril materials, not Raid Coins.

The sources do not name the raid boss of **89 Story Quests** (between Quest 614 and Quest 825): each takes the boss of the nearest quest with a known boss (rebuilt). For 24 quests the sources disagree: the community wiki's boss is used.

Some Story Quests always bring their boss when cleared, and here they do so **at every replay** (rebuilt: the sources do not say whether a replay brought it again).

The chance of meeting a rare (Ω) boss is read from the 2016 data up to Quest 555; from Quest 556 each boss keeps its most common 2016 chance (rebuilt). In two worlds whose raid background is incomplete, the fight shows the background of Daybreak Town.

### Event raids of 2016-2018 — older raid events, open all day

The older raid events are back too: one-off, weekend-only, monthly, daily and daily showdown raids. A weekend-only raid opens only on a real Saturday or Sunday (Pacific time), and a daily showdown shows the boss of the real weekday.

The Daily Raid Events keep their boss and their level rules, but not their hours: they are open all day, like every raid here (see **Raid AP and Bonus Time** above). Where an event had a male and a female Raid Board, both are open (not original) (the original showed only the one of your avatar).

Where no original value survives, the HP of these raids is the boss's 2016 HP scaled by date up to the few readings of 2018, and the three tiers of 2018 (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced) are inferred from one reading each (rebuilt). Timers and AP of further attempts come from the same boss's 2016 rows, or from the weekend rule (3 hours, 1 hour from May 2018).

Level rewards named in an event's notice reach your Presents when you defeat its boss at that level; rewards the notice does not detail are not given. Where an event's notice gives another level rule, it is followed: in the Daily Raid Events of December 2016 and in the Mysterious Sir raid of March 2018 the boss rises by **5 levels** at every defeat; in the Gear Golem raid of July 2018 and the Gummi Hound raid of August 2018 by 5 in Bonus Time. Three raid events of spring 2018 (Queen Bee, Crabby Cake, Gear Golem) only said that bosses level faster in Bonus Time: they rise by 5 too, and the Closehanded Captain week of September 10, 2018 by 2 like the next weeks (rebuilt: the amount is not stated). Bonus Time is on Saturday and Sunday here (see above). In the Daily Raid Events of July 2017 to January 2018 coins come every **5 levels**, up to LV 55 (LV 50 from November 2017), as their notices said; the coin amounts are (rebuilt).

## Variable Special Attacks

**IMPORTANT** · 10/21

Special Attacks whose damage depends on a condition (for example "Inflicts more damage the higher your HP") show their **damage range** again, as in the original game: the Medal details show "x7.12 - 8.64" instead of a single value, and in battle the damage follows the condition.

For 65 Special Attacks the original values are known and are used as they were. The other 642 condition-based Special Attacks of later Medals have their damage range too (rebuilt): the lowest and highest damage come from the community Medal list, and the way damage grows between them follows the battle formula of the original game.

The conditions are: HP, HP or defense paid, turns passed, attacks in succession, gauges full or used, gauges of the previous or next Medal, enemies defeated, Lux, a single enemy left, skills triggered, slot number, party size, and poisoned, asleep or paralyzed targets.

For some conditions no original attack of the same kind is known (gauges of the previous or next Medal, less Lux, skills triggered, lower slot, party size): the **curve** between the lowest and the highest damage is reconstructed from a similar condition, so the damage in between may differ a little from the original.

Special Attacks that are "more powerful when critical hit" (18) deal the lowest damage normally and the highest damage on a **critical** hit.

Before this change, about 100 condition-based Special Attacks dealt their highest damage every time, so they may now feel weaker when the condition is not met.

## Special Attack animations

**INFORMATION** · 10/20

Special Attacks use their **original animations** again, and the number of hits you see is always the one written on the Medal ("Deals 13 hits" = 13 hits). The total damage does not change.

For about 700 Special Attacks the original animation is known: it is used as it was. Some later Medals (for example "Ars Solum EX") use the animation of the original attack with the same name.

For the other Special Attacks the original animation is lost: they use the original animation of **another** Special Attack with the same number of hits (rebuilt). Support Special Attacks (healing, boosts) use the original support animations.

A few Special Attacks still use a simple animation, because their only copy is incomplete.

Supernovas deal 1 hit (4 for "Reversed Strike All M"), as in the original game. Which Supernova animation each Medal used is not known: each Supernova uses one of the original Supernova animations with that number of hits (rebuilt).

## Spirit Tricks

**INFORMATION** · 10/20

All 22 **Tricks** of your Spirit work in battle, unlocked by Spirit rank as in the original game: Steal I-III, Blast I-V, Esuna I, HP Recovery I, Poison, Paralysis and Sleep Boost I, P/S/M Attack Boost I-II and Combo I-III. Only the highest level of each Trick is active.

The original Trick data is lost: effects, ranks, charges and chances come from the community wiki, and how each Trick works in battle comes from the game itself. Some values are (rebuilt): the "low HP" limit of Blast (25% of the enemy’s HP), the strength of Poison Boost and Sleep Boost, the ailments cured by Esuna, and how many times per battle P/S/M Attack Boost can trigger (once per turn, with no battle limit).

Blast hits with a hidden attack that deals exactly its damage (10,000 to 750,000). Steal picks one enemy drop per battle (50% chance for each enemy that drops an item) and gives it x2, x3 or x4.

## Login Bonus

**INFORMATION** · 10/20

The **Login Bonus** is back: log in once a day for the Daily Login Bonus (a 5-day cycle: 100, 100, 200, 200 and 300 Jewels, the amounts used from 2017 to the end of the game) and the Total Login Bonus (Jewels and titles on the original milestone days, up to day 1850). The rewards arrive in your Presents.

The login day changes at **08:00 UTC**, together with the Daily Missions. In the original game it changed at 4:00 a.m. Pacific Time.

Missing a day does not restart the 5-day cycle: it goes on from the day you reached (reconstructed: the original rule is not documented).

Special Login Bonuses (campaigns with gifts on set dates) will come with the events.

## Shop

**INFORMATION** · 10/20

The Shop shows one week of your Story period; every banner is in the **Banner Catalog**.

Seasonal banners follow the **real calendar**, and you can keep one past banner.

### Overview — the Banner Catalog, guarantees and odds

The first banner in the Shop, **Banner Catalog**, cannot be drawn: tap its **Details** to see the banners and the Medals they contain. Its price is shown in **Purchased Jewels**, which cannot be bought on this server, so its draw button is greyed out and does nothing, like a deal already used up (not original).

Draws cost Jewels or Tickets earned in the game. **Draw Odds** shows the rates used by this restoration.

Guarantees work as on the original banners: the yellow line under a banner counts down the draws left to its guaranteed Medal.

When a guarantee covers too many Medals to name them all, the yellow line names the kind of Medal from the banner's original notice instead (for example "A SN or higher Medal is guaranteed! Draws left: 3"). This wording is written by this restoration.

Every original banner is in the Banner Catalog. Banners unlock as you progress through the Story; in the original game they followed the calendar.

### Weekly rotation — one week of your Story period

Each part of the Story matches a period of the original calendar. A period usually lasts **several weeks** (an example is in the notice «Differences from the original game»). The Shop shows **one week** of that period at a time: every banner that ran during that week, up to 20.

Every **Monday** the Shop moves to the next week; after the last week of the period it starts again from the first one, so every banner of your period comes back.

When you clear enough Story Quests to reach the next period, the Shop moves to the first week of the new period. Banners of periods you have passed stay in the Banner Catalog, where you can still see their Medals and rates.

Each banner's **Details** say whether it is in the Shop now and, if not, in how many weeks it comes back.

### Seasonal banners — real-calendar windows

During each of these windows of the **real calendar**, the most recent unlocked edition of that season's banners appears at the top of the Shop, right after the Banner Catalog (and the kept banner, if any), from the first time you log in inside the window until the first login after it ends. If no edition is unlocked yet, nothing appears.

Windows (UTC):

New Year: December 22 – January 17

Lunar New Year: January 28 – February 3

Valentine's Day: February 3 – February 17

Anniversary: April 6 – April 30

Summer: August 22 – September 1

Halloween: October 12 – November 2

Black Friday / Cyber Monday: November 22 – December 9

Winter Holidays: December 7 – January 6

When two seasons overlap, both appear, unless they use different special tickets (Spooky Cookies, Moogle Points, Holiday Draw Tickets): then only the first one in the list above shows. A seasonal banner that is already in the week's rotation is not shown twice.

### Keep one banner in the Shop — one past banner stays every week

In the Banner Catalog, tap **📌 Keep in the Shop** on a banner's row (or in its **Details**). It then stays in the Shop every week, right after the Banner Catalog plate and above the seasonal banners, marked with a **📌 KEPT IN SHOP** badge.

You can keep only banners from a period you have already passed in the Story. Daily Free Deals can be kept too (see the Free Deals notice). Other free draws and seasonal banners cannot be kept.

Keep one banner at a time: keeping another replaces the first, and **Remove from the Shop** takes it out whenever you like.

**Leave and re-enter the Shop** after keeping a banner to see it.

The banner's own draw limits still apply.

The Shop never shows two different special tickets at once. When they clash, the seasonal banner wins, then the kept banner, then the weekly rotation; the banner that loses steps aside until the season ends. A clash means a different special ticket (for example Spooky Cookies, Moogle Points or Holiday Draw Tickets).

### Catalog filters — find banners by year, Medal or price

The **GAME YEAR** tabs split the Banner Catalog by year, each year running from one anniversary (April 7) to the next.

The **Filters** panel has three parts: **Find a Medal** (the banners where it can be drawn), **Paid with** (Jewels, Tickets and so on) and **Show** (all banners, those in the Shop now, unlocked or still locked ones).

Filters only change the Catalog, never the Shop.

## Traits

**INFORMATION** · 10/19

Fusing two copies of the same Medal gives it a random trait, as in the original game. Copies of different rarities of the same Medal count too. One fusion gives one trait, even with several copies; Special Attack dots and the Special Attack Bonus still count every copy.

When all trait slots are full you choose which trait to replace, or tap **Skip** to keep your traits. If you close the game before choosing, the choice comes back the next time you log in.

The chances of each trait were never published: here **each of the 11 traits has the same chance** (reconstructed). The stronger traits added in 2020 (Extra Attack: 50% Power, Enemy DEF -70%, STR +1500, Damage in Raids +50%) are available **only from the Moogle Shop**.

Evolved versions count as the same Medal: SN, SN+ and SN++ of the same character, or a Medal and its Supernova, share their Album slot and give each other traits (reconstructed from the original deals).

**Trait Medals** are back: grey 6★ Medals that belong to one Medal and share its Album slot. Fused onto their Medal they give a trait and one Special Attack dot, or unlock or re-roll the Special Attack Bonus when the dots are full; fused onto other Medals they only give EXP. Their pictures are recreated in black and white from their Medal, and their EXP and sell price are those of their Medal (reconstructed).

## Medal skills

**IMPORTANT** · 10/18

Medal skills are reconstructed: 25 of the 163 skills use the original values, the other 138 are rebuilt from the community skill list (power and max trigger rate from the wiki, other values estimated). Some long descriptions are shortened.

After each mission, the Skill EXP of the battle goes to every Medal in your deck that has a skill. The original rule is not known, so this one is (rebuilt).

Combo skills marked "& GA 0", "& GA 1" and "& GA 2" make the special attack cost 0, 1 or 2 gauges; when the skill is checked is our estimate, as no original value was found.

Scrooge Medals from the **Skills to Thrill** deals and from the VIP! Skill quest come with the skill shown on their banner, at max level. Other new Scrooge Medals get a skill picked by rarity, because the original skill depended on where the Scrooge came from. Scrooges you already own keep their skill.

## About game data updates

**IMPORTANT** · 10/16

Every Monday and on the 1st of each month, the game downloads updated game data (missions and events).

The first time you open the game after an update, loading takes longer than usual. Please keep the app open until the home screen appears. Later logins are fast again.

If the app is closed during the download, nothing is lost: the download simply starts again the next time you open the game.

## Missions

**INFORMATION** · 10/16

Open the **scroll** under your avatar on the home screen to see your Beginner, Daily, Weekly and Limited Missions, then tap **Receive** to collect the rewards.

Daily Missions reset every day, Weekly Missions every Monday and Limited Missions with their campaign: the time left is shown on each tab.

Limited campaigns come back in turn. A campaign that gives a Medal joins the rotation only once your Story reaches the date it ran in the original game (for example the "Defeat 100 enemies!" campaign with SN++ - FF7R Aerith, April 2020).

Rewards that you can own only once (Medals, titles, Spirit parts) are given once: when their campaign comes back, the missions you already collected are not shown again. Missions you did not finish in time come back, and finished rewards you did not collect go to your Presents.

## Supernova and Dual Meow Wow

**INFORMATION** · 10/14

Equipping a Supernova Medal lets you unleash its powerful Supernova once per quest: tap the button under the special attack gauge during your turn.

To unlock or raise the Supernova of a Medal, use Evolve with five identical Dual Meow Wow Medals; the base Medal must be at its max level.
